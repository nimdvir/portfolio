
# **And Now, Seriously**

**Forget everything you knew about Jim Carrey and welcome the improved model: idealist, populist, and spiritualist. "The American economy is a rotten apple," he says. "The worship of money is making us stupider than ever." But he also has some interesting insights about himself: "I was stoned, I was wiped out, I was everything. Now I just want to be so clear, to turn into a ball of light and fly off the planet." Indeed, unedited.**

*By Nimrod Dvir, New York | Published: 18.01.06*

---
![J](https://res.cloudinary.com/dkndq6lyz/image/upload/v1763929039/jim-carrey_jqfnzq.jpg)

Once they told us that crime doesn't pay. On the other hand, they once told us that Jim Carrey is totally crazy. Starting this week, both of these concepts are about to change: welcome "Fun with Dick and Jane," a fine social satire disguised as a holiday movie.

From afar, "Dick and Jane" looks like another family comedy where Jim Carrey mostly makes faces: The story is about Dick (Carrey) and Jane (Téa Leoni), a suburban couple who undergo a surprising economic crash. In their distress, the two are forced to rob stores and embark on a Robin Hood-esque journey to reclaim their self-respect. It sounds light, but up close you discover it is nothing less than an advertisement for anarchy: The film joyfully shouts the benefits of crime, while slinging poisonous arrows at Botox, foreign workers, George Bush, and other stale topics in modern-day America.

Like the movie, Carrey, 43, is surprising up close. Sitting next to Leoni at the Waldorf Astoria Hotel in New York, the comic genius chooses to forgo the familiar rubber face and goes for spreading social messages instead: "The movie deals with a very serious subject in a funny way," he opens. "Think about the story—two people deteriorate to the poverty line, dragged into the world of crime—in an instant, this could have turned into a tear-jerker."

**So what were you actually trying to say?**

**Carrey:** "The situation today is that the corporate American economy is a rotten apple, and we all nibble on it as long as we don't get sick. There is an exposed nerve in our country—the widening class differences and the worship of money are making us stupider than ever. Once, a CEO earned 40 times more than the simple worker; today he earns 400 times more. It's simply vulgar! It takes me back to the days of the Roman Empire."

**Hello, you also calculate your salary in millions.**

"Money is great, but it is a symptom, not the goal. I never worked for the paycheck. I knew that if the day came when I received a payment of 10 million dollars, it wouldn't mean I have money—it would mean I have the power to create what I really want."

Welcome the 2005 model of Jim Carrey—with power, vision, and mostly post-"Dumb and Dumber" era. Don't expect jokes from him, at least not off-screen. The Carrey of today is a budding philosopher, a poet of clichés, although his observations are much more amusing: "I'm a clarity freak now," he answers when asked what gives him the strength to continue. "And my energy source is meditation. I was stoned, I was wiped out, I was everything, and now I just want to be so clear that I turn into a ball of light and fly off the planet."

**Without spoiling the ending, the couple isn't really punished for all the robberies they commit. Is this what happens when you're 'clear'—you understand that crime is great?**

"It's not that the movie says 'Okay, you're having a hard time financially, pick up a gun and go rob a bank.' I see it more as a statement that in today's situation, to survive, you need to know how to bend the rules."
![J](https://res.cloudinary.com/dkndq6lyz/image/upload/v1763929039/jim-carrey2_hnhz8s.jpg)

**"Dick and Jane" is based on a 1977 movie, originally with Jane Fonda. Why do a cover version specifically now?**

"It's true there is a remake disease right now, but this isn't 'The Godfather' that people automatically know. We thought this is a movie that fits the 21st century, with everything happening with Enron (a large American company that crashed in a similar way to the company shown in the film) and with our President, on whom Alec Baldwin, my boss in the movie, based the character. It was important to me to make a movie that also has a message, that presents our economy in a slightly different light."

**Do you remember periods when you were poor yourself?**

"Of course! I once worked as a janitor and security guard at a steel factory. I was 16, and my dad had just become unemployed. I went to school during the day, worked at night, and mostly I was an angry guy all the time. I was angry at what the universe did to me and my dad. Since then, I clean my own toilets; I understand the suffering of janitors."

Much water has flowed under the bridge since the janitor days. Carrey, who broke into consciousness as a gifted comedian in films like "Ace Ventura" and "The Mask," began developing himself simultaneously as a serious actor. In "The Truman Show," he moved us as a man who falls victim to the world of reality TV; in "Man on the Moon," he stepped into the shoes of legendary comedian Andy Kaufman; and in the beautiful "Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind," he discusses the connection between love and memory. Now, as mentioned, he is going for the middle ground: on one hand, delivering a laugh-out-loud comedy, and on the other hand—poverty isn't exactly a Brothers Grimm fairytale.

**You've come a long way since your days as Ace Ventura.**

"I am evolving and proud to reveal different sides in diverse projects. I am very proud of 'Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind' because I think we changed something in cinematic language with it. In the same way, I am also proud of 'Dumb and Dumber,' which also has its own place. I feel that I am changing something in my work, certainly."

**Besides the message, what did you enjoy most about filming the movie?**

"I loved all the creative ways we find to disguise ourselves during a robbery. For instance, there is a part in the movie where we put these voice distorters on ourselves so they won't recognize us. It was just hilarious—everything you say sounds funny! That scene is entirely improvised, and afterward, we sat until 4:00 AM laughing with those synthesizers. Another funny part was when we dressed up as Sonny and Cher. Originally, Téa was supposed to be Cher and I was Sonny, but when the costumes arrived we said, 'Why don't we actually switch?' Téa fit Sonny's clothes perfectly. I, on the other hand, was a very ugly Cher."

**For most of the movie, you are attached to Téa. Did you work a lot on the relationship before filming?**

"The truth is the first time I met Téa was seven hours before filming. For me, coming to filming without rehearsals is like coming to the first day of school without pants. Luckily, we had a spark from the first moment. Originally, the romantic element between the characters wasn't supposed to be emphasized at all. The love of Dick and Jane explicitly comes from us. Téa rolls with my nonsense. My jokes just bounce off her, and vice versa. She is a comedically flexible girl, very cooperative."

**In the movie, part of what drives the characters is who has the nicer car, the more prestigious job, etc. Do you also feel the pressure of 'who has the bigger one'?**

"There's nothing you can do, the neighbor's grass always looks greener. We have a bit in the movie where my character steals grass, literally, from the neighbors. What's funny is that this week I saw a report on the news about a pair of dentists who stole grass from neighbors. Apparently, it's less far-fetched than we thought."

**A whole interview has passed and not a single word about private life. Is it not your cup of tea to bring the media home?**

"I am very lucky that I didn't become one of those tabloid things. Yes, there are photographers who follow me, but luckily they never dragged me into that mud of gossip. Not that there is anything wrong with exposure, I love exposing myself (laughs), but my personal life is a private matter. There isn't too much pressure on me to share—there is so much media today that people are just desperate to be noticed and willing to do anything. With me, in any case, love is not for sale. Besides, I don't go out at all."

**Why actually?**

"People say to me 'Let's go to a club,' and I say 'Let's sit at home and expand our consciousness.' I sound like a psychopath, right? But my life is like an experiment. I am always looking for answers—reading about new things, trying new things with food, trying new people, and also not afraid to experiment with movies. Today, my main expression is in movies. I used to paint, and now I do cinema—drama, comedy, sculpture, painting—it's all the same thing. For me, the taste of life is not making millions, or starring in a sex scandal that feeds the newspapers. For me, the peak of ambition is to be a creative human being."
